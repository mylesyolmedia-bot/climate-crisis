Climate Change in California

Extreme Heat

Rising temperatures are making heat waves more frequent and severe across California, leading to greater rates of death and illness, increased exposure to diseases, and worsening air and water quality. All of these combinations bring significant economic consequences for California.

Article source: https://climateresilience.ca.gov/overview/impacts.html

  • According to a report from the state insurance department, several hundred deaths have occurred from seven heat events between 2013 to 2022.
  • In addition, $7.7 billion in damages was caused due to lost wages, agricultural and manufacturing disruptions, power outages, and more.
  • Source: https://shastascout.org/hundreds-of-deaths-thousands-of-injuries-billions-of-dollars-the-cost-of-extreme-heat-in-california/

Wildfires

Intense heat dries out vegetation, creating conditions that ignite and spread wildfires more rapidly. These fires cause widespread displacement, death and injury of people, damage ecosystems, and drive major economic losses.

Article source: https://climateresilience.ca.gov/overview/impacts.html

  • Thousands of wildfires have occurred in California every year, along with 35 "mega-fires" that have occurred since 2000.
  • In 2020, the largest wildfire in California, the Augustus Complex wildfire, occurred, burning 1,032,648 acres, destroying 935 structures, and causing 1 death.
  • Source: https://www.frontlinewildfire.com/wildfire-news-and-resources/california-wildfires-history-statistics/

Air Quality

The smoke produced by these fires poses serious health risks, particularly to vulnerable groups such as children, elderly people, pregnant women, and people with heart or lung conditions. These groups face a risk of harm from prolonged exposure.

Article source: https://ehs.stanford.edu/wildfire-smoke

  • According to the California Air Resources Board, over 90% of Californians breathe unhealthy levels of at least one type of air pollutant each year.
  • Source: https://trackingcalifornia.org/topics/air-quality#gsc.tab=0
  • You can view the air quality in your area through this website: https://www.airnow.gov/state/?name=california

Droughts

Droughts place a big strain on the agriculture industry in California. Although warmer climate does bring some benefits for some other regions in California for agriculture, those are outweighed by the disadvantage of water scarcity and invasive pests.

Article source: https://water.ca.gov/Programs/All-Programs/Climate-Change-Program/Climate-Change-and-Water

  • Droughts are part of a climate pattern in California called "weather whiplash". In one period of time California has heavy rainfall, and in another severe droughts. Droughts can cause degraded drinking water quality, poor air quality, and increases exposure to fungus.
  • Source: https://oehha.ca.gov/climate-change/epic-2022/changes-climate/drought
  • You can learn more about droughts in California through this website: https://caseagrant.ucsd.edu/faq-droughts-californias-coastal-regions

Water Supply

Droughts reshape California's water supply. Because of climate change, precipitation falls as rain rather than snow, leading to snowpack to be reduced. This means that there is less stored water for communities that may rely on snowpack water through the dry seasons.

Article source: https://water.ca.gov/Programs/All-Programs/Climate-Change-Program

  • A major source of California water is the snowpack from mountains; the Sierra Nevada snowpack has provided about 15 million acre-feet of water, which is about a third of California's water supply.
  • The Sierra Nevada snowpack is projected to experience a 48-65% loss from the historical April 1 average by the end of the century.
  • Source: https://water.ca.gov/Programs/All-Programs/Climate-Change-Program/Climate-Change-and-Water

Sea Level Rise

Rising seas level threaten the Coastline of California. Coast communities face erosion, contamination with saltwater getting into freshwater sources, and losses of California beaches.

Article source: https://water.ca.gov/Programs/All-Programs/Climate-Change-Program

  • Since around 1925, there has been a trend for a steady rise in sea level. Warmer sea water temperatures and melting ice has contributed to the increasing sea level. The impacts involve more flooding and erosion for the California Coast.
  • Source: https://scripps.ucsd.edu/research/climate-change-resources/faq-sea-level-rise-and-california
  • You can view the effects of rising sea levels through this website: https://coast.noaa.gov/slr/
